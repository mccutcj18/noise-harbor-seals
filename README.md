### Abstract
Pollution, like in-air noise, is a threat to pinnipeds whose habitat heavily overlaps with areas of human development. The amphibious nature of pinniped hearing abilities exposes them to in-air noise, putting them at risk of threshold shifts, acoustic masking, and behavioral disturbances. Due to their sensitive hearing while out of the water, harbor seals (Phoca vitulina) are particularly susceptible to anthropogenic in-air noise. Hauled-out harbor seals alter their behaviors or numbers in response to in-air noise disturbance. Yet, to our knowledge there has been no effort to establish whether a similar pattern occurs while seals are in the water with their head above the surface. To address this gap, we compared the number of surfaced seals and the duration of time they spent at the surface (surfacing duration) to in-air noise at the Bellingham Waterfront in Washington State, USA. We used Generalized Linear Mixed Models with a Bayesian framework to determine whether in-air noise predicted the number of seals and/or their surfacing durations. There was no significant relationship between in-air noise levels, or the other predictors, and the numbers of seals present at the waterfront or the surfacing durations. Our results suggest that the harbor seals who frequent the study site tolerate the local in-air noise levels. Given that the site is heavily used by humans, future work should examine if this tolerance puts seals at risk of harmful interactions with other human activities.
